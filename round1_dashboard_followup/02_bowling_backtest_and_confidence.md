# Stage 02 · Challenge the bowling baseline and measure what is reliable

## Agent prompt

Run a narrowly scoped bowling experiment on saved, validated delivery data from the revised organizer pack. Keep the recovered baseline and approved shortlist from Stages 00–01 available. Fit and score real alternatives using actual observed outcomes. The key question is whether a different forecast or a relaxed role constraint would make better auction selections on later matches.

### Freeze the comparison before running it

1. Reuse the verified delivery ledger and the existing innings-state and player-match-phase aggregation. Validate the ledger hash against the manifest, exclude non-regulation or unusable events per the current rules, and preserve missing outcome evidence. Use legal-ball denominators, credit bowler wickets correctly and retain competition tier, phase and cricket-context attribution. Aggregate once and reuse across fits. Cache only by raw/ledger hash, source version, configuration and cutoff.
2. Reproduce the retained bowling forecast first. Its final path combines pooled cricket features, ridge and shallow histogram boosting with regularized opposition/venue adjustment. Use actual code for weights and features. Keep the current target of 0.75 times pressure impact plus 0.25 times ordinary impact on a common per-12-legal-ball scale, with pressure and ordinary impact measured against comparable situations. Keep pressure impact and the player's pressure-minus-ordinary change separately for evaluation. Scheduled role or roster assumptions stay out of match-outcome labels. Point forecasts and the rule that turns them into an RR shortlist can be evaluated independently.
3. Run two challengers plus the reproduced baseline.
   - **Recency-weighted IPL baseline.** Compute per-split contextual impact per 12 legal balls from prior IPL observations with a 365-day half-life. Weight matched event contributions and legal-ball support consistently, including legal-ball and extras attribution already handled by the ledger. Combine the pressure and ordinary rates with the frozen target weights. For a group with no history, use a documented pre-cutoff role/phase peer rate to form the prediction and label the evidence level. Keep the cohort and assessment target common.
   - **Shrunk pressure uplift.** Learn the usual-performance component and the pressure-minus-usual component separately. Use small-sample partial pooling toward the training-only role/phase peer uplift, for example `estimated uplift = (n_eff × player uplift + k × prior uplift) / (n_eff + k)`. Base support on both pressure and ordinary exposure, such as their harmonic mean. Then estimate future composite impact from a regularized ordinary forecast plus 0.75 times the pooled uplift. If tuning peer support strength or ridge regularization, use a small declared grid and earlier training-only folds. Compare against the already implemented split-target challenger where it helps interpret what changed. Avoid counting the same evidence twice through multiple penalties.
4. Fit at exactly the same historical cutoffs for all candidates. Use forward windows from 2024–25 for comparison, 2026 as a previously viewed retrospective check, and the 2025 and 2026 dated historical auction pools. Each cutoff uses only prior features, outcomes with closed windows and contemporaneous pool information. Fit any scaler, peer prior or imputer within the training fold. Reuse the chronological control for the final snapshot and record actual training-row counts _per recipe_ rather than reusing one model's count. Test fit invariance to perturbations of later results and verify no fixture can contribute to both the fold's features and outcomes. [Rolling-origin evaluation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html)
5. Compare the same frozen scores under the standard role policy and score-only policy, holding recruitment pools constant. Present a soft role-balance challenger only if a documented cricket-based role choice and narrow tuning plan were recorded before the test. Report coverage of spin, new-ball, middle-over and death roles alongside overall utility. Test actual choice pairs Jadeja versus Anukul Roy, Boult versus Mitchell Starc, and Jadeja versus Deepak Chahar in the no-quota variant. A useful 2026 statistic for one player can coexist with a different multi-season forecast. Show recent samples, phases, recent vs longer-run impact and omission sensitivity. Avoid hand-picking names or thresholds in response to the outcome.

### Assess and decide

- On identical assessable player-window rows, report MAE, realized pressure and composite impact per selected slot, number assessed out of ten and, where supported, average impact among assessed picks. Show both because slot impact divides the sum of observed contributions by ten even when some picks lack outcome coverage. Missing evidence is unknown. Add counts and paired by-window differences. Do not turn four replay windows or overlapping player histories into an exaggerated sample size.
- Keep separate tables for forward validation, 2026 retrospective replay and actual historical auction pools. The baseline's 2026 bowling auction replay was about −0.89 composite impact per offered slot with four assessed picks. Reproduce the baseline and show that limited coverage. Prior score-only comparison left both historical auction results unchanged. Prespecify the adoption rules. Reuse the existing promotion rule (at least +0.15 selection impact gain, pressure and MAE guardrails, minimum coverage and year-by-year checks) when the target and units match. An auction improvement should be visible on a meaningfully assessed sample and consistent with forward evidence. A tie, isolated improvement or gain bought by losing exposure does not justify a change.
- For each eligible promoted snapshot recompute selection stability with the proper candidate pool, role policy and fixed, logged seed. Resample whole fixtures within the existing season/tier strata, preserve duplicate sampled matches as multiplicities, and recompute aggregates and selection. If fitted coefficients remain fixed, say so. Report per-player inclusion counts, whole-list count and useful overlap/marginal diagnostics. Scope resamples to the actual snapshot being displayed. Maintain the current 1,000 draws where applying the same metric. Uncertainty about future release, injury and opportunities is separate.
- A direct probability of playing well is optional and requires a defined event, enough historical chronological labelled examples, held-out Brier score against prevalence and a calibration check. For example, “positive pressure impact once the player has the required observed workload” is a possible conditional event. State its scope and skip the probability if sample or calibration fails. It cannot represent likelihood that RR will buy the player or that the player is the single best fit. A point forecast, a past observed impact, a role-fit reason and a repeat-selection frequency are different evidence types. Use the true 42.8% or any measured successor value. Do not tune to a 90% resampling number.
- If an alternative passes, generate a new versioned evidence bundle, a comparable raw-model ranking from Stage 01 and paired RR output. Keep the old snapshot. Record who changes, why, and the comparison. Update portal spellings, pressure samples, role-scoped metrics, uncertainty, credits and any newly required portraits before using the successor in the primary boards. If no alternate passes, keep the fully reproduced baseline, log the test result, and show the strongest close calls and limitations.

### Loop and handoff

Reproduce the control, run the dated comparisons, inspect per-window metrics and model output, repair any leakage or selection mismatch, and rerun. **Pass** with a reproducible and justified keep-or-promote decision, frozen source and training metadata, equal-cohort metrics, and an explicit uncertainty interpretation. A well-evidenced decision to keep the baseline satisfies this gate. Open [Stage 03](03_methodology_and_evidence.md).
