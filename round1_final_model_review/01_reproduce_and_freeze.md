## Implement

1. Fingerprint all seven competition inputs, archived outputs, the market evidence bundle, the current code or source-tree digest, relevant config, dependency versions and seed. List exactly which archived artifacts each manifest covers. Verify that the form worksheet, shortlist top ten and both role exports match by ordered role and canonical player ID.
2. Map the pipeline entry points from manifest and ingest through ledger, split, pressure, impact, forecast, compare, robustness, ranking and export. For each stage record inputs, primary key, output grain, date cutoff, downstream users and rebuild cost. Establish a baseline run in a new directory or prove an existing baseline is byte-identical where its dependencies match. Record timing by stage. Classify scorecard anomalies as verified, reconstructed or unresolved.
3. Write the working competition contract from the case and entrant form evidence. Specify the ordered ten batting and ten bowling ranks, the report evidence required, uniqueness within each role, role and ID verification, verified live labels and RR purpose. Keep the report-ready evidence linked to one canonical ordered export. Confirm the entrant's two decisions for the review run in its config: RR retain options eligible, and preference for more Indian players with role fit. A bid-only view is a comparison scenario. Any official restriction on form entries must come from the case, live form evidence or written organizer guidance.
4. Freeze the unit of prediction before experiments. At the auction-time selection origin, predict an upcoming IPL player's performance under pressure for a declared role/phase deployment. Higher adjusted runs per 12 legal balls is favorable in both disciplines. The matching ordinary forecast uses the same deployment mix, and pressure lift is the difference. State exactly which realized opportunities support the score, how a missing or short opportunity is represented, and which forecast question a 12-ball scenario answers. Record the 48-ball/six-match career floor, 24-ball/two-match IPL floor, and current raw positive-rate policy as project baselines. Track changes to these as explicit decision-policy alternatives.
5. Freeze the experiment budget and adoption rule in a machine-readable protocol. Use the saved versions as controls. The latest code's Q25 loss tolerance is 0.05 and its legacy shortlist improvement hurdle is 1.0 run per 12 on 2025. Reproduce those numbers as a control. Record a meaningful improvement margin, acceptable calibration deviation, exposure and role coverage requirements, and a paired-comparison decision rule before the new trials. A proposed criterion change requires a rationale recorded independently of individual player outcomes. Prefer the simpler reproducible candidate when the data cannot discriminate.
6. Use match-level chronology. Inventory the eligible seasons and the availability of complete as-of roles, labels and match outcomes. Use earlier rolling origins for fit tuning, the 2025 selection comparison as reused validation, and 2026 as an inspected retrospective. Any newly sourced test set needs dated, authorized, nonoverlapping observations and a fixed protocol before inspecting its outcomes. Describe the strength of evidence that is actually available. Distinguish code and export correctness, forecast calibration, rank stability, and future auction eligibility throughout.
