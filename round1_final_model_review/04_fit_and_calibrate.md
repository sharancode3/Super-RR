# Stage 04 — Fit and calibrate the candidate models

## Decide what to fit

Reproduce Candidate B batting, B0 bowling, RPP and C-IPL on Stage 02 data, using the Stage 03 runner. Preserve the already-tried 0.75 blend and endpoints as controls. Read the complete fit and scoring paths in rr_pressure/forecast.py, challengers.py, role_challenger.py and delivery_challenger.py. Record how each candidate estimates the mean, spread, pressure change and IPL transfer. Use the same role and phase definitions across competing models.

## Primary targeted challenger

1. Train one IPL-anchored role-and-phase partial-pooling challenger per discipline, reusing RPP or C-IPL components where their targets match. At the player-match-phase level, a suitable starting model is adjusted impact per 12 legal balls predicted by role-by-phase and innings baselines, a shrunk player ordinary effect, and a pressure interaction comprising a role-by-phase effect plus a shrunk player pressure effect. Fit IPL, T20I and emerging evidence using an explicit competition term and an IPL-first weight. Constrain the number of interactions. An individual player-by-phase pressure effect requires adequate exposure and a stronger penalty. Include a season or pre-cutoff recency term where earlier replay supports it.
2. Use available exposure and match information to prevent a cameo or high-volume match from dominating. With a weighted objective, use one row per declared opportunity and a documented exposure cap and match contribution. Distinguish observed balls and matches from weighted support. If reporting Kish effective sample size, compute (sum of weights) squared divided by sum of squared weights and state its unit. Use match clustering for inference. Apply stronger pooling to thin IPL samples or discordant competition rates. Compare IPL-only with a declared moderate T20I weight and the existing RPP default. Treat the weights as predictive design choices and give their validation trade-off.
3. Use a small predeclared grid for role/player penalties, recency and competition weights. At most one primary model family and one targeted diagnostic per discipline, plus the existing controls. Reuse the existing hyperparameters as defaults. Use earlier IPL OOF loss and calibration to select a candidate and evaluate the frozen candidate on the reused 2025 window and 2026 retrospective. If existing RPP already solves the measured issue, a corrected replay of that model is sufficient. Preserve evidence of unsuccessful experiments.
4. Score ordinary and pressure predictions for the same intended IPL role/phase and innings mix. Draw mixes from pre-cutoff IPL deployment, pooled toward role weights where a player has limited direct evidence. Declare the mixture and show sensitivity for alternative plausible RR usage. Compute mean pressure impact and pressure lift in consistent units. If aggregating over a distribution of contexts, derive a predictive quantile on the stated mixture with calibrated residual draws or a coherent fitted distribution. A weighted average of phase quantiles is a forecast combination and needs its own calibration.

## Batting short-opportunity diagnostic

5. Reproduce the audit with all positive-exposure pressure opportunities. Count one-to-five-ball stints, dismissals, innings endings and other causes. Keep the observed opportunity mechanism in view. If the short-outing gap persists, reuse the DHP delivery model as the targeted diagnostic and fit a run component and a discrete striker dismissal hazard on pre-ball context with role and player pooling. Candidate features are balls faced before the event, role, phase, innings and competition. Treat a not-out end as censoring where appropriate. Distinguish off-strike ends and nonstriker run-outs from striker dismissals. Use probability and exposure weights that match the declared estimand.
6. For a simulated 12-ball deployment, simulate or integrate legal events and survival until a dismissing or innings-ending transition. Make the result explicitly either total contribution over the simulated opportunity or a rate per 12 legal balls conditional on exposure. Evaluate against matching observations or report the simulation as a scouting view. Compare Q25 loss and coverage for all positive opportunities and the 1–5 and 6-plus strata. Preserve the strongest supported batting model if the hazard diagnostic helps only one slice.

## Quantiles and efficiency

7. Fit the uncertainty layer on strictly earlier OOF residuals. Start with regularized role/workload residual scale or empirical residual quantiles, pooled to the global discipline calibration for sparse groups. Fit the lower quarter and optional 80% prediction interval with a declared method. Check out-of-time coverage, interval width, interval score, bias and finite ordered quantiles. Describe a prediction interval as future performance variation and a confidence interval as uncertainty around a measured quantity. Compute actual empirical coverage. Reserve any probability of outperforming an incumbent for a well-defined paired estimand with a calibration check.
8. Fit each candidate once per cutoff, seed, target and hyperparameter key. Reuse match/phase aggregates and fitted predictions across selection rules. Use sparse design and a numerically stable solve. Where covariance computation dominates, solve for needed projected variances and verify parity before replacing a dense calculation. Cache with input, code, cutoff and config fingerprints. Log time and peak workload before and after optimization. Reuse existing libraries and functions.

## Loop

Train the predeclared configurations, compare paired held-out results, and diagnose failures by role, workload, calibration and competition. On a defect in target or cutoff, return to Stage 02 or 03 and invalidate affected caches. On a weak or tied model, record the result and retain the best supported candidate. Exhaust the declared trials only if they answer distinct questions about the measured gap.

## Exit and redirect

Save a reproducible fitted candidate or baseline decision for each discipline, with out-of-time metrics, uncertainty diagnostics and runtime. The retained forecast must have a declared estimand and its calibration status. Continue to [Stage 05](05_shortlist_uncertainty.md).
