## Research and what transfers

- The 2026 T20 preprint uses context-adjusted run residuals, state-dependent dismissal costs, and partial pooling. CAMP adds opponent and situation context. Apply these ideas using strictly prior estimates and the pack's actual coverage. [T20 impact preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18020v2) · [CAMP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13700)
- The 2024 T20I chase study uses required scoring and resources. It supplies a chase sensitivity and helps distinguish urgency from leverage. First-innings match value needs its own logic. [T20I pressure index](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.3233/JSA-240792)
- The Stevenson–Brewer study uses a changing dismissal hazard and censoring in Test cricket. Borrow the statistical structure and verify the behavior on T20 opportunities in this pack. [Batting hazard study](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssc/article/70/2/481/7033927)
- Pathak et al., published 10 November 2026, use LightGBM and an MLP with contextual data to construct T20I XIs. This motivates a regularized tree diagnostic with matched features. Its usefulness for an IPL pressure forecast requires our out-of-time evaluation. [T20I role-based lineup study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-75023-z)
- Use rolling-origin evaluation and proper scores for quantiles, intervals and binary events. Time-dependent coverage and individual probabilities require empirical checking. [Rolling origins](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169207000000650) · [Proper scoring rules](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)
