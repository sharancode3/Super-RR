## Fit the primary challenger

4. Extend the existing role-and-phase partial-pooling approach before writing a new framework. Fit a shared role/phase baseline with a regularized player level and a regularized player pressure effect. Include appropriate competition and era offsets, and an innings or phase interaction with pressure where enough matches support it. The simplest useful form has mean `role_phase_baseline + player_level + pressure * (role_phase_pressure + player_pressure)`. Use a robust likelihood or a weighted penalized fit with an empirically estimated predictive spread. Shrink player effects toward comparable IPL roles and give sparse tiers less influence. Record priors or regularization, training cutoffs and effective match-level evidence. The release or trade status is a decision input, outside this skill fit.
5. Handle workload-dependent tails explicitly. A simple starting variance is `match_variance + delivery_variance / legal_balls`, with parameter uncertainty added where supported and learned on earlier blocks. Fit with observed workload in the likelihood. At prediction time, fix a relevant planned opportunity or integrate over a distribution of workload and phase fitted from earlier matches. For a mixture, solve the mixture CDF for Q25 and interval bounds. The weighted mean of separate quantiles generally differs from the mixture quantile. Keep predictions ordered, finite and stable for new or lightly observed players.
6. For batting, require the simple challenger to account for the observed 1–5-ball slice. If its earlier-block residuals still show material early-dismissal bias, test one two-part extension using the existing delivery-hazard challenger as a starting point. Fit run contribution and dismissal hazard jointly or with linked role and player terms. A dismissal ends future batting exposure and an innings ending without a dismissal censors the opportunity. Evaluate survival or censoring against observed stint endings and simulate or integrate the predicted opportunity distribution only from information available at prediction time. Use genuine events, clearly labeled simulated predictive draws, and common realized evaluation targets. Recover the same batting-impact units. Reproduce the prior hazard experiment as context. Its 2025 short-stint diagnostic improved loss and its old shortlist result did not earn promotion.
7. For bowling, compare the pressure-neutral B0 control and an IPL-anchored role/phase forecast with an added pressure effect. Use credited wickets, runs conceded including valid charged extras, and the expected deployment phase. Support new-ball, middle-over and death forecasts where data allows. Require additional value from personalized pressure effects on the same evidence cohort. If added effects are weakly identified, retain the simpler fitted control for evaluation.

## Control model complexity and implementation cost

Use earlier cutoffs for features, validation, stopping and calibration. Use the same workload policy and common rows. A blend is justified only by earlier out-of-fold evidence of complementary errors and a stable small weight search. Keep only candidates that add evidence for the decision.
9. Fit reproducibly with the frozen seed. Cache delivery-derived observations and cutoff features by input hash, cutoff and recipe. Reuse the fitted contexts and fold predictions across candidate and policy evaluations.
